// Your operating cycle

Four phases, from log yard to shipped order.

A sawmill is divergent yield from a variable raw material, not a factory with clean bills of material. Here is where the information breaks down at each stage, and what changes when it does not.

01Supply & Intake02Breakdown & Recovery03Drying & Grading04Cost & Margin
01

Supply & Intake

Buy · receive · scale · log yard

Your raw material is variable before you own it and variable after. What arrived, what was scaled and what was invoiced are three different records held in three different places, and the gaps between them are usually absorbed into a variance account rather than explained. Meanwhile the log yard is either short or carrying cash you did not mean to carry.

// Automated reconciliation

Quarri matches what arrived to what was scaled and what was invoiced, so log cost is right before a single board is cut.

  • Paper log receipts and tallies read several times daily
  • Arrived, scaled and invoiced matched per lot
  • Differences surfaced, not absorbed into variance
  • Yard cover by species, weeks ahead

02

Breakdown & Recovery

Saw · trim · resaw · tally

Recovery is the number the whole mill turns on, and it moves with species, diameter, season and operator. Overrun is real: more tally out than log scale in is a fact of the saw rather than an error. The trouble is that the station capping your week is rarely the one everybody watches, and nobody can prove which it is while each line keeps its own sheet.

// Bottleneck detection

Quarri reads utilisation and downtime across every station, so the step actually capping your week is named rather than argued about.

  • Recovery tracked by shift, line and log supplier
  • Overrun reconciled rather than hidden
  • Downtime attributed to the station causing it
  • The real constraint named, not assumed

03

Drying & Grading

Kiln · dry · plane · grade

A kiln is measured in dwell days, not hours, and it runs whether or not anyone is watching. Between green, on sticks, kiln-dried, rough and dressed, the same pack changes identity four times, and most mills can tell you what they own in total but not what state it is in or how long it has been sitting there.

// Grade recovery analytics

Quarri tracks every pack through green, kiln-dried and dressed, so inventory is a position you can act on rather than a total you can only report.

  • Every pack tracked through green, dried and dressed
  • Kiln dwell measured in days, per charge
  • Grade recovery and percentage to premium, by run
  • Ageing stock visible before it becomes dead

04

Cost & Margin

Cost · price · reconcile · ship

Log cost goes in at one end and a lumber price comes out of the other, and in between sit drying, planing, grade and the orders you chose to run. Mills know their margin in aggregate. Very few can say which products, which species and which customers actually earned it, so the two decisions that matter most, what to cut next and what to charge for it, both get made on instinct.

// Margin intelligence

Quarri joins log cost to finished tally and to the price you achieved, so what to run next and what to sell it for stop being two separate guesses.

  • Log cost carried through to finished tally
  • Margin by product, species and customer
  • Lumber priced against where the market is
  • Purchase price variance caught in the cycle

Purchase orders & inventory

Order · route · pick · promise

An order for kiln-dried dressed stock can be met from finished inventory, by planing rough stock you already hold, by drying green stock first, or by cutting it from logs. Each route carries a different lead time, a different cost, and a different consequence for the next order. Most mills choose by habit, because working it out properly takes longer than the answer is worth.

// Smart Scheduler

Quarri prices every available route against real inventory and real capacity, so the promise you make is the cheapest one that still hits the date.

  • Every available route costed and timed per order
  • Inventory checked by state, not as one total
  • Lead time taken from the route, not a flat rule
  • What the next order needs is weighed too

// In practice

A sawmill and remanufacturing operation

// Before

Production tallies, shift reports and maintenance notes were written on paper and keyed once a month, so the mill was managed on numbers that were already several weeks old.

// With Quarri

Quarri reads the daily tallies and shift reports as they are written, reconciles log scale to sawn tally, and tracks every pack through drying and dressing. Reading downtime properly showed 78% of it sitting on the trim line rather than the saw, and a join fix recovered roughly half of a finished inventory of over 15M board feet that had not been fully attributed.
