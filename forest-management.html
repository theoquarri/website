// Your operating cycle

Four phases, from standing timber to settled account.

Every forest operation runs the same cycle, whatever the licence and whatever the ground. Here is where the information breaks down at each stage, and what changes when it does not.

Plan & Sell

Cruise · forest inventory · bid · standing sale

A cruise is a sample taken on a date, and the stand has not stopped growing since. Some of those surveys are a year or more old. Growth outruns the estimate in one block and falls short in another, and the evidence to tell which is which sits in a legacy system nobody can refresh cheaply. Meanwhile you are bidding and selling against that picture as though it were still true.

// Where Quarri works

Quarri refreshes an ageing cruise against what the ground actually yielded, so you bid on the stand you have today rather than the one you surveyed last year.

  • Ageing surveys refreshed against delivered volume
  • Estimate against actual, block by block
  • Bids priced on real supply, not a stale cruise
  • Stand movement carried into the margin picture

02

Harvest & Measurement

Fell · skid · measure · cut log

Who took what, from where, and under which entitlement. That question is a compliance obligation before it is an operational one, and the record answering it is usually paper: cut logs, stand reports and contractor dockets that were never designed to be compared with each other. On top of that, the weather decides when crews can work at all, and frozen ground and spring breakup move volumes far more than crew performance does.

// Where Quarri works

Quarri reads your paper cut logs and ties every load to its block, licence and entitlement, so the compliance record builds itself as you cut.

  • Paper cut logs and stand reports made comparable
  • Every load tied to block, licence and entitlement
  • Volume cut reconciled against bid and estimate
  • Contractor splits preserved exactly as written
  • Operability rated daily, a fortnight ahead

03

Haulage & Delivery

Load · way slip · haul · deliver

Who moved what, where to, and on whose account. The answer arrives as way slips and bills of lading, mostly on paper, in volume, and often weeks after the load ran. The hard part is not the haulage itself but the allocation: a single load can carry entitlements for a landowner, a contractor and a levy, each governed by a different rule, and those rules change more often than anyone would like.

// Where Quarri works

Quarri splits every load by rule rather than by hand, so who is owed what stays consistent across thousands of way slips and changes the moment you change the rule.

  • Paper way slips read in volume, never keyed
  • Complex splits applied by rule, not by hand
  • Rules edited by your team, no release needed
  • Hauler pay from the record that proves it

04

Scale & Settlement

Scale · accrue · reconcile · release

This is where the whole cycle has to add up. Scale data proves the volume, the levy accrual turns it into a liability, and an invoice arrives from the authority months later to be matched against it. Compare at the month total and it ties. Compare at the load and a timing difference, a rate error and a load that never should have been charged stop looking alike.

// Where Quarri works

Quarri matches the accrual to the invoice at bill-of-lading level, so a rate error stops looking like a timing difference and nothing unmatched disappears into a monthly total.

  • Accrual drafted from live scale data
  • Matched per bill of lading, on your tolerances
  • Anything unmatched ages in view, never absorbed
  • Release written back as a postable file
