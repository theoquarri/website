What does the forestry software market look like in 2026?

Theo Leslie · 4 min read

← Software and systems

Nobody agrees on its size. Published estimates for 2025 range from $1.48 billion to $2.89 billion, and forecasts for the early 2030s range from about $3 billion to nearly $13 billion, with arithmetic that doesn't always hold together. What the reports do agree on is that software is still sold by job, for forest management, inventory, harvest and logging, and mapping. One report says those tools are becoming interoperable platforms. For a buyer, the question that matters is whether that interoperability reaches the records where the money is: scale tickets, contracts and the accounts.

What the usual answer says

The pages at the top of a search for this question are market-research summaries. Each opens with a size and a growth rate, lists drivers such as cloud, AI, GIS and sustainability reporting, and names a set of leading vendors. The Business Research Company, for example, attributes growth partly to "rising adoption of AI-driven forest analytics". None of the pages compares its figures with the others'.

Five estimates of one market

360iResearch, in a report sold through Research and Markets and published in January 2026, says the market "was valued at USD 2.89 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach USD 3.49 billion in 2026", growing at 23.78% a year to $12.88 billion by 2032.

The Business Research Company puts 2025 at $1.48 billion, growing to $1.72 billion in 2026 and $3.09 billion in 2030, at 15.8% a year. Verified Market Reports, in a page last updated in July 2026, gives the same three numbers but a forecast year of 2032. The arithmetic settles which fits. At 15.8% a year, $1.48 billion reaches about $3.08 billion after five years and about $4.13 billion after seven, so the 2032 version doesn't agree with its own growth rate.

SkyQuest values the market at $1.43 billion in 2024 and $1.58 billion in 2025, growing at 10.2% to $3.43 billion by 2033, while its own snapshot box gives a market size of $1.3 billion. The snapshot lists cloud-based deployment as the fastest-growing segment. Its text then says: "Being the leader in cloud-based deployment, on-premises deployment is the fastest-growing segment for 2024". Strategic Market Research puts 2024 at $3.5 billion, more than twice SkyQuest's figure for the same year.

The spread has a cause worth knowing. The reports draw the boundary of forestry software differently, and they rarely publish their method in enough detail to check. Verified Market Reports describes its source only as "aggregated industry datasets & trade analysis". The 360iResearch company list mixes forestry tools with tree-care apps and environmental health and safety software.

What the reports say about structure

The reports segment the market by application, such as forest management, logging management and harvester software, and by deployment. The 360iResearch report goes further. It says "architectural shifts toward modular, API-first platforms are enabling interoperability across complementary systems", so that customers can "assemble best-of-breed stacks", and that this raises "the importance of interoperability, data portability, and vendor neutrality in procurement decisions".

That may be true between forest-side tools, such as inventory, mapping and harvest planning. None of the reports sizes the work that sits between those tools and the rest of the business: matching tickets to contracts, inventory to scale, and harvest to the accounts. Where that work is still done in spreadsheets, an API on each tool doesn't do it by itself.

How to read the market for a decision

Map it by job, not by size. List the jobs the business runs software for, the tool that does each, and the records it holds. Then mark each place where data moves between tools by hand, and ask each vendor which of those moves its connectors actually make. That tells you more about what to buy next than any growth rate.

When it doesn't apply

Investors and vendors need a headline figure, and for them the right step is to buy the reports and compare their methods and boundaries directly. Businesses running a single integrated system may have few gaps to map. And the reports' vendor lists are a reasonable starting point for a shortlist, if not for a market size.
