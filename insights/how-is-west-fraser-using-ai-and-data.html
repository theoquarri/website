How is West Fraser using AI and data?

Theo Leslie · 4 min read

← Listed companies and AI

Its filings name no AI project, but outside them the company is piloting autonomous log hauling and hiring a team to build AI tools. The annual MD&A first mentioned AI for 2025, in a risk section that refers to AI initiatives and expected benefits without naming any. The board added oversight of AI risk to its audit committee charter in September 2025. In May 2026, the autonomous trucking company Kodiak announced a pilot hauling timber from forest sites to one of West Fraser's facilities. In September 2026, West Fraser advertised for developers to build AI assistants and agents. What the filings do describe is data work: a strategy to "integrate and unify" multiple information technology platforms, which any such tools will depend on.

What the usual answer says

Descriptions of West Fraser's technology say it uses AI and data for sawmill optimisation, predictive maintenance, log scanning and supply chain planning, as part of investment in automation. The company's own filings don't present these as an AI programme.

What the filings say

The 2024 annual MD&A contains no mention of AI or artificial intelligence. The 2025 annual MD&A, filed in February 2026, adds a section headed "Artificial Intelligence (AI) Adoption, Utilization and Governance". It is set out as a risk factor, and it says AI technologies "are rapidly transforming business operations, competitive dynamics, and customer expectations". It warns that "Failure to adopt AI at a pace consistent with industry developments" could hurt competitiveness. Talent shortages could "limit our ability to implement AI initiatives as planned". It notes that "Effective AI deployment depends on" data infrastructure, "process integration, and appropriate management of data throughout its lifecycle". It closes by saying these risks "may limit the benefits we expect from AI". So initiatives are planned and benefits expected, but none is named or quantified. The Q2 2026 interim MD&A does not mention AI.

The 2026 information circular adds the governance history. In September 2024 the board "participated in a multi-day session on management's approach to artificial intelligence within the business". On 10 September 2025, the audit committee charter was amended "to capture identification of risks related to artificial intelligence and other new or emerging technologies".

What sits outside the filings

Kodiak's announcement of 7 May 2026 says it "will pilot the use of its self-driving technology to transport timber from forest sites to one of West Fraser's" processing facilities "later this year", with results used "to inform any future long-term deployment". That is the vendor's account of a pilot, not a result.

In September 2026, West Fraser posted an AI Agent Developer role. The job is to "Design, build, test, deploy, and support AI-powered applications, assistants, agents, and workflow solutions", and it combines "software engineering, generative AI, data integration, solution testing, and production support". A second role covers "platform engineering, cloud operations, identity and security, responsible AI, governance, observability, cost management, and production support". A company hiring to build and govern its own AI tools is past the stage of merely assessing the risk.

The data story underneath

The more telling passage is in the 2025 MD&A's information technology risk section. The company says "the history of our operations has resulted in multiple information technology platforms and applications across our business operations". That, it says, "complicates our business controls and processes, including our internal controls over financial reporting". Its "strategy is to integrate and unify these information technology systems" in order "to optimize our finance, sales, inventory management, maintenance and business intelligence functions". The same language appears in the 2024 MD&A.

An AI agent that answers questions across mills needs those functions on a consistent footing first. The hiring and the unification strategy point at the same work.

What to watch

In the filings, the move from risk factor to programme would show as an initiative named in the strategy or capital sections, with a benefit attached. Reported progress on unifying the platforms would be another sign. Pilot results from the hauling trial would be another.

When it doesn't apply

Filings show what a company chooses to disclose, and pilots below materiality rarely appear. Private sawmill groups disclose nothing comparable, so this kind of reading works only for listed companies.
